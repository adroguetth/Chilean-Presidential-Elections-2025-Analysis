## Key Findings Summary

### 1. Decisive Kast Victory with a Broad Mandate
- **58.24%** of valid votes vs **41.76%** for Jara (communal database, 346 communes)
- Official Servel result: **58.16%** vs **41.84%** (incorporating overseas vote)
- Margin of **16.48 percentage points** and **more than 2 million votes** (2,046,992)
- Won in **all 16 regions** and in **310 of 346 communes** (89.6%)
- Victory margin without precedent in the history of Chilean presidential runoffs since the return to democracy in 1990

### 2. Unprecedented Territorial Hegemony
- Kast won **89.6% of communes**; Jara only **36 communes (10.4%)**
- **~71%** of Kast's communes were won with **more than 60%** of the valid vote (220 of 310)
- Urban-rural gradient: Kast vote share rises systematically as commune size decreases
- **Three political worlds** articulated: anti-establishment North, Santiago elite and rural-conservative southern Chile

### 3. Explosion of the Protest Vote (Null/Blank)
- Null vote: **+421,458 votes (+116.9%)** between first and second round
- Reached **5.85%** of ballots cast (highest level in a Chilean presidential election since the return to democracy)
- Pearson correlation between Parisi vote (1st round) and null vote (2nd round): **r = 0.681** — the strongest of the three correlations tested
- Null vote concentrated in Parisi strongholds of the **Norte Grande** (Antofagasta reached 9.04%, Atacama 8.05%)

### 4. Compulsory Voting and the New Electorate
- Participation increased **+60.4%** (8.3M → 13.4M votes cast)
- Kast nearly **doubled** his vote count (3.6M → 7.2M, +97%)
- The left grew by only **+587,000 votes** (4.6M → 5.2M, +13%)
- Left lost **~14 percentage points** in valid vote share (55.87% → 41.84%)
- The asymmetry confirms that newly incorporated voters distributed significantly more favourably to the right

### 5. Electoral Realignment 2021–2025
- **56.1%** of communes changed winner between 2021 and 2025 (194 of 346)
- **158 communes** passed from Boric to Kast — 81.4% of the left's 2021 territorial base lost
- Kast retained **100%** of his 2021 communes (152 communes, +158 new ones)
- The left retained only **18.6%** of its 2021 communes (36 of 194)
- A realignment of this magnitude — affecting more than half of all communes — has no precedent in prior Chilean presidential runoffs

### 6. Destination of the Parisi Vote
- **90.6%** of communes where Parisi won the first round (58 of 64) were won by Kast in the second round
- Jara flipped only **6 Parisi communes**, all in the northern mining belt with strong trade-union presence
- Transfer was **incomplete**: a significant fraction opted for the null vote rather than validating a ballot
- Segmentation via **k-means clustering** (4 clusters): near-complete transfer to Kast in the conservative centre-south; active protest (null vote) in the Norte Grande; fragmentation in intermediate cities; partial transfer to Jara in mining communes with high labour organisation

### 7. Geography of the Victory
- **Kast's electoral heartland**: southern and centre-south Chile (La Araucanía, Los Lagos, Maule, Biobío, Ñuble — all communes won, margins frequently exceeding 20 pp)
- **Jara's strongholds**: Metropolitan Region (21 of her 36 communes, 58% of total) and northern mining enclaves
- **North**: greatest Kast percentage-point growth (Arica y Parinacota +36.47 pp; Tarapacá +35.21 pp)
- **South**: consolidation with maximum stability (La Araucanía: 0% of communes changed sign between 2021 and 2025)

### 8. Resistance to the Protest Vote
- Kast grew between **+38 and +34 pp** even in communes with the highest protest vote increase
- Difference of only **~4 pp** between the lowest- and highest-protest commune groups — protest voting had a near-symmetric dampening effect on his growth, not an asymmetric one
- Jara **lost absolute votes in zero communes** between first and second round
- But she **failed to expand** beyond her traditional strongholds: zero communes flipped from Kast or other right-wing candidates

---

### Global Strategic Implication

The 2025 election was not a simple presidential transition but an **electoral realignment of historic magnitude**. Kast constructed a **territorially diverse coalition** articulating the anti-establishment North, the Santiago elite and rural-conservative southern Chile. The left was **confined to an archipelago of urban strongholds** with no capacity to expand towards provincial Chile. **Compulsory voting** incorporated millions of new voters whom the right captured with markedly greater efficiency. The elevated **protest vote** reveals a structural anti-establishment discontent that was not fully absorbed by either final option — and whose management will be one of the defining challenges of the incoming government.
