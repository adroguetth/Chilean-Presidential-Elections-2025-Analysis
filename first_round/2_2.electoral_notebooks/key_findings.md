## Key Findings: Electoral Analysis – First Round Presidential Election 2025

### Executive Summary

The first round of the 2025 presidential election, held under mandatory voting, shaped a map of advanced fragmentation and structural realignment. **Jeannette Jara (26.75%)** and **José Antonio Kast (23.96%)** advanced to the runoff, while **Franco Parisi (19.71%)** emerged as the great arbiter of the ballotage. Territorial analysis reveals a sharp fracture between urban-popular Chile (Jara), rural-conservative Chile (Kast), and the anti-system North (Parisi). Mandatory voting brought **5.1 million new voters** into the electorate, fundamentally transforming its composition.

---

### 1. Institutional Context: Mandatory Voting

- **Turnout:** **13.39 million** (+89.1% vs. 2021)
- **Null votes:** **2.69%** (vs. 0.78% in 2021)
- **Blank votes:** **1.06%** (vs. 0.43% in 2021)
- **Null-to-blank ratio:** **2.5:1** (vs. 1.8:1 in 2021)

The return of mandatory voting was the most disruptive factor. It brought millions of citizens with low levels of political engagement to the polls, who leaned overwhelmingly toward anti-system and right-wing options.

---

### 2. National Results by Candidate

| Candidate | Votes | % | Status |
|-----------|-------|-----|--------|
| **Jeannette Jara** | 3,446,854 | 26.75% | Runoff |
| **José Antonio Kast** | 3,086,963 | 23.96% | Runoff |
| **Franco Parisi** | 2,550,770 | 19.71% | Eliminated |
| **Johannes Kaiser** | 1,796,034 | 13.94% | Eliminated |
| **Evelyn Matthei** | 1,603,104 | 12.44% | Eliminated |

**Key finding:** No candidate exceeded 27%. Fragmentation is structural, not circumstantial. The combined right (Kast + Kaiser + Matthei) surpassed **50% for the first time** in the voluntary-voting era — a threshold already hinted at in 2021 (Kast + Sichel ≈ 40.8%) but only consolidated in 2025.

**Evolution 2021 → 2025 by candidate** *(added finding)*:

| Candidate / 2021 reference | 2021 Votes | 2025 Votes | Change |
|----------------------------|------------|------------|--------|
| Parisi | 899,067 | 2,550,770 | **+184%** |
| Matthei (ref. Sichel) | 894,838 | 1,603,104 | +79% |
| Kast | 1,954,607 | 3,086,963 | +58% |
| Jara (ref. Boric+Provoste) | ~2,610,258 | 3,446,854 | +32% |
| Enríquez-Ominami (MEO) | 533,554 | 154,321 | **-71%** |

MEO’s collapse is the most severe of the cycle: his 2025 vote fell well below even his 2013 result (10.99%), reflecting the exhaustion of independent left-wing candidacies.

### 3. Territorial Mapping: Winners by Commune

| Candidate | Communes Won | % of Total |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| **Kast** | 169 | 48.8% |
| **Jara** | 105 | 30.3% |
| **Parisi** | 64 | 18.5% |
| **Matthei** | 4 | 1.2% |
| **Kaiser** | 4 | 1.2% |

**Structural paradox:** Kast won more communes (169) but placed second in votes (23.96%). Jara won fewer communes (105) but led the vote (26.75%). Kast controls rural and peripheral Chile; Jara controls urban and densely populated Chile.

---

### 4. Territorial Trifurcation: Three Electoral Chiles

| Territory | Candidate | Regions | Logic |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|
| **Anti-system North** | Parisi | Arica, Tarapacá, Antofagasta, Atacama | Mining extractivism, migration crisis, state abandonment |
| **Urban-progressive Center** | Jara | Coquimbo, Valparaíso, Metropolitana | Population density, services, social organization |
| **Center-South rural-conservative** | Kast | O'Higgins, Maule, Ñuble, Biobío, La Araucanía, Los Ríos, Los Lagos | Rurality, territorial conflict, perception of abandonment |

**Swing regions:** Coquimbo (0.77 pp), O'Higgins (1.01 pp), Biobío (2.00 pp), Los Ríos (2.44 pp). These four regions define the battlefield of the runoff.

---

### 5. Urban-Rural Gradient

| Commune Size | Dominant Candidate | % of Controlled Population |
|--------------|--------------------|----------------------------|
| >200,000 inhabitants | **Jara** | **76.9%** |
| 100,000–199,999 | Jara | 59.5% |
| 50,000–99,999 | Jara | 61.5% |
| 10,000–49,999 | **Kast** | **57.9%** |
| <10,000 inhabitants | **Kast** | **~60%** |

**Inflection point:** Around **50,000 inhabitants**. Above that line, Jara dominates; below it, Kast dominates. This demographic boundary defines the fracture between urban and rural Chile.

---

### 6. Regional Capitals: Inversion of the Power Map

| Candidate | Capitals Won | % | Regions Won | % |
|-----------|--------------|-----|-------------|-----|
| **Jara** | **10/16** | **62.5%** | 5/16 | 31.3% |
| **Kast** | 4/16 | 25.0% | **7/16** | **43.8%** |
| **Parisi** | 2/16 | 12.5% | 4/16 | 25.0% |

**Paradox:** Capitals "vote differently" from their own regions. They are more favorable to Jara, less favorable to Kast, and impervious to Parisi outside the North. **Iquique** (0.77 pp margin, with Jara, Parisi, and Kast virtually tied) was the most competitive capital in the country.

---

### 7. Protest Vote: Null and Blank Votes

#### Null Vote (2.69%)

- **Highest intensity:** Norte Grande (2.92%) and Norte Chico (3.02%)
- **Lowest intensity:** Centro (2.67%) and Centro Sur (2.56%)
- **Patagonia:** 3.16% (highest macrozone); **Easter Island (Isla de Pascua)** registered 5.54%, the highest null-vote commune in the country.
- **Territorial relationship with Parisi:** The macrozones with the highest Parisi vote (Norte Grande and Norte Chico) are also those with the highest null vote, suggesting that a fraction of the anti-system electorate combines both forms of rejection — though this does not necessarily imply the same voter in each case (see correction note below).

#### Blank Vote (1.06%)

- **Highest intensity:** Patagonia (1.49%) and Norte Chico (1.37%)
- **Lowest intensity:** Centro (0.99%)
- **Kaiser (median 2.5%):** Anomaly — the libertarian electorate in the periphery leans toward passive protest.
- **Matthei (median 0.5%):** Traditional right was the most disciplined.

**Extreme case:** **Antártica** recorded 0% null votes *and* 0% blank votes — out of 77 valid votes, none were invalid. It is the commune with the highest electoral discipline in the country (won by Kast with 45.45%). *(added finding)*

---

### 8. Parisi: The Anti-System Phenomenon

- **Votes:** 899,067 (2021) → 2,550,770 (2025) **(+183.7%)**
- **Communes won:** 6 (2021) → 64 (2025)
- **Retention rate:** 95.7% — the most loyal bloc in the electoral map
- **Territorial core:** Norte Grande (>40% in 13 communes)

**Typologies of the Parisi vote:**
- **Border communes:** Ollagüe (58.17%), Colchane (57.30%) — isolation, migration crisis.
- **Mining communes:** María Elena (45.86%), Alto Hospicio (42.94%) — centralized extractivism.
- **Rural northern communes:** Punitaqui (41.07%), Alto del Carmen (43.16%) — water crisis.

**The duopoly’s ceiling** *(added finding)*: In 11 communes (3.2% of the national total, all in Norte Grande and Norte Chico), Parisi surpassed **Jara and Kast combined**, with an average lead of 8.9 pp over the sum of both — Ollagüe (+29.97 pp), Colchane (+23.34 pp), General Lagos (+12.64 pp). There, the anti-system vote was not the third force: it was the first, by a wide margin.

**Conclusion:** Parisi is not a passing protest vote. He is a structural force with its own territorial base.

---

### 9. Realignment 2021→2025

- **Communes that changed:** 122 out of 271 (45%)
- **Parisi:** retained 22 out of 23 communes (95.7%)
- **Boric→Jara:** 50 out of 58 (86.2%)
- **Kast:** retained 127 out of 180 (70.6%)
- **Provoste:** retained 0 out of 11 (0%) — total collapse of the center-left

**Cannibalization from Kast to Parisi:** 25 communes of Kast shifted to Parisi according to the general transition matrix. *(Correction note: The source analysis of the 41 new communes won by Parisi reports 26 communes coming from Kast, not 25 — a minor discrepancy between two cuts of the same data unresolved in the source. In any case, the qualitative reading does not change: between two-thirds and slightly more of Parisi’s territorial expansion came from Kast’s electorate, not from the left.)*

**Unequal volatility** *(added finding)*: Most realignment concentrated in the most urbanized regions — Valparaíso (87.1% of communes changed winner) and the Metropolitan Region (81.6%) — while Antofagasta and La Araucanía recorded no change at all (0.0%). Mandatory voting reshuffled the board mainly where density and social heterogeneity were already high, not in rural Chile, where 2021 alignments proved nearly irreversible.

### 10. Vote Transfers (Baseline Scenario)

| Eliminated Candidate | Transfer Assumption |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| **Kaiser** | 95% → Kast, 5% abstention |
| **Matthei** | 55% → Kast, 20% → Jara, 25% abstention |
| **Parisi** | Not assigned — uncertainty factor |

**Baseline projection (without Parisi):** Kast 58.4% — Jara 41.6% (margin: 1,643,236 votes)

The Parisi vote is the only factor that can change the equation. Without it, Kast wins comfortably; with it distributed favorably toward Jara, the result becomes competitive. These figures are transfer assumptions, not observed results: only the runoff itself will reveal which scenario came closest to reality.

### 12. Recoverable Communes (Margin <1,000 votes)

| Candidate | Communes | Votes Needed |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **Jara** | 81 | 33,999 |
| **Kast** | 53 | 17,720 |

**Key asymmetry:** Jara needs nearly twice as many votes as Kast to flip communes. The concentration of Jara’s recoverable communes in O’Higgins, Maule, and Valparaíso confirms her weakness in intermediate Chile.

**The tightest margins in the country** *(added finding)*: **San Vicente** (O’Higgins), where Kast lost by just 11 votes, and **Río Ibáñez** (Aysén), where Kast lost by just 6 votes against Jara — the most competitive commune in the entire election.

### 13. Runoff Projections

| Scenario | Jara | Kast | Margin |
|----------|------|------|--------|
| **Baseline (without Parisi)** | 41.6% | 58.4% | +1,643,236 |
| **Optimistic Jara** (60% Parisi→Jara) | 49.5% | 50.5% | +112,774 |
| **Neutral** (50% Parisi→Kast) | 42.1% | 57.9% | +1,898,313 |
| **Optimistic Kast** (60% Parisi→Kast) | 40.0% | 60.0% | +2,408,467 |

**Conclusion:** The four modeled scenarios — differing only in the assumed transfer of Parisi’s vote — place Kast with a structural advantage in the majority of cases. However, that advantage is not insurmountable: in the most favorable scenario for Jara, the margin narrows to just **112,774 votes**, within the range that differential mobilization could reverse. The real unknown heading into the ballotage is not whether a structural advantage exists, but its magnitude — and that depends entirely on transfer assumptions that no first-round data can verify with certainty.

### 14. Strategic Political Reading

**For Kast:**
- Structural advantage from the start: unified right-wing base (>50% in the first round)
- High ideological compatibility with Kaiser’s electorate; thematic affinity (order, migration) with a portion of Parisi’s electorate in the North
- Risk: leakage of the Matthei sector toward abstention or blank voting (baseline scenario assumes 25%)
- Key: consolidate the conservative vote, contest the anti-system North, moderate discourse on value-based issues

**For Jara:**
- Cohesion of the progressive bloc (86.2% Boric→Jara retention)
- Opportunity: capture Parisi’s vote in the North and Centro Sur
- Risk: wear and tear of the Boric administration; perception of continuity
- Key: break with continuity, connect with pragmatic voters, focus on concrete solutions

**For Parisi:**
- The most loyal base in the electoral map (95.7% retention)
- 64 communes won; own parliamentary bench elected on the same day as the first round
- His position heading into the runoff — strategic silence, support for a finalist, or promotion of null/blank voting — remained publicly undefined at the close of this analysis; any of the three paths is coherent with his identity as an anti-system "third pole"
- Key: maintain autonomy as a political asset, consolidate territorial structure ahead of 2029

---

### Methodology

- **Source:** Official Servel data (2021 and 2025), aggregated at the level of Chile’s 346 communes (continental and insular)
- **Unit of analysis:** 346 communes, 16 regions, 7 macrozones
- **Approach:** Territorial, demographic, and sociological
- **Indicators:** Absolute and relative votes, margins, gaps, retention rates, transfers, urban-rural gradients
- **Limitations:**
  - Mandatory voting (2025) dilutes direct percentage comparisons with 2021 (voluntary voting); priority is given to the analysis of absolute votes and territorial evolution.
  - National figures exclude votes from Chileans abroad (not assignable to a commune), so they may differ by tenths of a point from the final official result proclaimed by Servel.
  - Two metrics in the realignment section (Parisi retention and Kast→Parisi communes) present minor discrepancies between different cuts of the same data; these are noted in the text where applicable.
  - All runoff projections (sections 10 and 13) are scenarios built on transfer assumptions, not observed results.
